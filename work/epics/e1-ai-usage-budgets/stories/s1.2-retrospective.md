## What Went Well

- Skipping formal `/rai-story-design` for this Simple-complexity story
  (per its own gate) and capturing the one real design decision — check
  all 4 scope dimensions, not just `user_id` — directly in `plan.md` kept
  this story fast without skipping rigor.
- Mirroring `governanceClient`'s exact mock-with-default-then-override
  testing convention for `spendCheck` (persisted as PAT-S-005) meant zero
  friction integrating a second gate into the same route.

## What to Improve

- Nothing significant — this story was small and the pattern from S1.1
  (real client conventions grounded via reading actual code first) carried
  over cleanly.

## Heutagogical Checkpoint

**1. What did you learn?** That skipping `/rai-story-design` for genuinely
simple stories (per the skill's own complexity gate) doesn't mean skipping
the design *decision* — it just means it lives in `plan.md` instead of a
separate document. The decision (check all 4 scopes) still needed to be
explicit and tested, just not in its own file.

**2. What would you change about the process?** Nothing for this story
specifically — the "Simple → skip design" gate worked exactly as intended.

**3. Are there improvements for the framework?** No new ones beyond what
S1.1 already surfaced (PAT-S-001).

**4. What are you more capable of now?** A validated template for wiring
a second enforcement-style gate into an existing route without disrupting
the first one's tests or conventions — directly reusable for S1.3's
`PUT /:id` wiring.

## Patterns Added

- **PAT-S-005** (process): mirror an existing route's mock-with-default
  testing convention when adding a second cross-cutting check, rather than
  inventing a new one.
